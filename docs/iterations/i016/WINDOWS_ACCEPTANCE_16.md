# Windows sea trial: Iteration 16, The World Awaits (0.103a)

A practical walkthrough for the Captain, about an hour: open the world chart,
plan a voyage abroad, sail it in Long Passage time with a stop on the way, and
arrive somewhere warm. Tick what works and note what does not, ideally with a
screenshot (Windows + Shift + S).

Nothing here has been tried on Windows yet. The automated checks ran on Linux
(headless and under a virtual display); they are not a Windows acceptance.
This trial is the acceptance.

**Get the project:** open the GitHub branch `claude/friendly-fermat-dey23f`,
choose **Code → Download ZIP**, extract it, open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7
and press **Run** (F5). Keep a copy of your saves folder before you start
(main menu → Load → the folder button shows where it is).

## 1. Old saves

- [ ] **Load** a game saved with 0.102b (or 0.102a). It opens normally; the
  main menu shows **Version 0.103a**.
- [ ] A boat at sea is still at sea on its way; a boat in harbour is still in
  harbour. Money, boats, stores, fish, renown and the Captain's Log are
  unchanged.

## 2. The world chart

- [ ] Open the chart. Press **Whole world**: the whole planet is shown, with
  the oceans and continents named, and only the larger harbours marked (none
  sitting on top of another).
- [ ] Zoom in on Europe: from about the size of the Mediterranean the familiar
  European chart takes over; zoom further into Denmark and the detailed Danish
  coast appears as before.
- [ ] Pan east across the Pacific: the chart wraps round at the Date Line with
  no seam in the land, the wind arrows or the grid.
- [ ] Find the new harbours with the search box: **Las Palmas**, **Mindelo**,
  **Castries**, **Colón**, **Panama City**, **Puerto Ayora**, **Taiohae**,
  **Papeete**, **Bora Bora**, **Suva**, **Auckland**, **Honolulu**.
- [ ] Panning and zooming feel smooth at every zoom (note the computer if not).

## 3. Plan a voyage abroad

- [ ] In Copenhagen (or anywhere in Europe), plot a course to **Las Palmas**:
  it leaves Danish waters by sea, rounds the coasts and never crosses land.
- [ ] Look at **Passage readiness**: it is friendly advice only; it says the
  forecast does not reach the whole passage and suggests stocking up.
- [ ] Try a course from **Castries** to **Panama City**: it goes through the
  Panama passage. A course from **Suva** to **Avarua** crosses the Date Line
  the short way.
- [ ] A waypoint dropped on land is refused with a clear message; no straight
  line is drawn across land.

## 4. Stock up and set sail

- [ ] At the grocery store, **Stock up** fills food and water for the boat's
  long-passage stores (Arctic Wanderer 32 days, Wing 36, Frogleap 34, Clam 45)
  and charges for the extra days.
- [ ] Set sail for Las Palmas. On deck a **Long passage** panel appears (bottom
  right): position and day at sea, distance and ETA, speed and wind, the
  outlook, the stores and the boat.
- [ ] Press **12 h/s**: the days go by quickly; the boat moves on the chart.
- [ ] Long Passage time stops by itself, with a reason in the panel, for a
  weather warning, a fish on the line (if the line is out), low stores, a
  boat-care notice, the hat in strong wind, and on arrival.
- [ ] **Chart** and **Save…** in the panel stop Long Passage time first. Save
  in mid-ocean, load the save: the boat is in the same place, still underway.
- [ ] Fish, cook, sleep and make coffee on the way as usual.

## 5. A harbour on the way

- [ ] Arrive in **Las Palmas** (or another island harbour): the boat is
  moored, the gangplank is out, you can walk ashore.
- [ ] **Go Ashore** offers a moment (sometimes one that belongs to the
  Atlantic islands). The grocery store works.

## 6. Onward to Paradise

- [ ] Sail on across the Atlantic to the Caribbean, through Panama and across
  the Pacific to the Marquesas and **Papeete** (Long Passage time makes this an
  evening, not a week). The Arctic Wanderer can do it with stops; the longest
  leg (Galápagos to the Marquesas, about four weeks) needs full passage stores.
- [ ] In Papeete: moored, gangplank out, Go Ashore works.
- [ ] The Captain's Log shows the new firsts as you earn them: beyond Danish
  waters, the first international destination, the first offshore crossing,
  across the Atlantic, into the Pacific, French Polynesia (and New Zealand if
  you go on). Each pays a little renown once.

## 7. Nothing old is lost

- [ ] Sailing in Danish and European waters is as before; the eight-direction
  view, the anchor, fishing, cooking, the companions, the hat and the
  boatyard all work; there are no large visible activation circles.
